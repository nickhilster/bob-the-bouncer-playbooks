Canadian scam guide

Pause. End it. Check for yourself.

If a call, text or email pressures you to pay or share information, stop and check through a contact you find independently.

See the four scams
  1. Pause. Don’t pay or share details under pressure.
  2. End it. Hang up. Don’t reply or open links in the message.
  3. Check. Use an official website, app or a number you already trust.

Four scams to recognize

The organizations below explain how to check for yourself.

Someone says you owe the CRA

A caller claims you owe tax and threatens arrest if you don’t pay now.

What to do: End the call. Check your balance in your CRA account or use contact details you find on Canada.ca. Don’t assume the call is real because the caller ID looks right.

  • CRA: Verify who called
  • CRA: Check a balance or payment

Someone says your SIN is suspended

A caller claims your Social Insurance Number was used in a crime or is being suspended. They ask you to confirm it.

What to do: Don’t give them your SIN. Hang up and look up Service Canada’s SIN Program yourself.

  • Service Canada: Recognize the suspension scam
  • Service Canada: Get help if your SIN was shared

Someone threatens your immigration status

A person claiming to work for Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) threatens deportation unless you pay or share personal details.

What to do: End the conversation. Don’t give them your file number or send money. Find IRCC’s contact options or check your application on Canada.ca.

  • IRCC: Recognize fraud targeting newcomers
  • IRCC: Find official contact options

A text says Canada Post is holding your parcel

A message says a delivery is on hold until you pay a fee through a link.

What to do: Don’t use the link. Open the official Canada Post website or app yourself to check tracking and any duty or taxes.

  • Canada Post: Check suspicious texts and emails

Already paid or shared information?

  • If you sent money or shared payment details, contact your financial institution right away using its official number.
  • Save messages and receipts without opening suspicious links. If you were a victim, contact local police.
  • Report the attempt to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre. You can report it even if you didn’t lose money.
